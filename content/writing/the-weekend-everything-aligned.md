Friday night, my mother was sitting in the audience at the Toronto Botanical Garden while I walked up to receive the 2026 Jamaican Diaspora Impact Award.

The same woman who brought me to this country when I was five. The same woman who watched me grow up in Jane and Finch, who watched me chase an idea with no money, no connections and no blueprint. She was in the room. Sold-out room. And I looked out at her before I said a word.

That was Friday.

![Dwayne Holness at the 2026 Jamaican Diaspora Impact Awards](/images/writing/weekend-aligned-jdia.jpg)

## Three days I didn't plan

Saturday, I put on a different suit and walked into the Fairmont Royal York for the 4th BlackNorth Initiative Excellence Gala, where I received the CIBC Entrepreneurship Award. Sunday morning, I went to church for Pastor Appreciation Day at KCLC. I went to celebrate other people. I walked out holding a Certificate of Appreciation for leading our media team.

Friday. Saturday. Sunday. Back to back to back.

I didn't schedule that. Nobody coordinated those three organizations. Two of the honours were confirmed weeks apart, and the third was a complete surprise. When I sat with it on Sunday night, I couldn't call it a coincidence. It was divine timing. God lined up three rooms in three days and let me walk through every one of them.

![The CIBC Entrepreneurship Award, BlackNorth Initiative Excellence Gala 2026](/images/writing/weekend-aligned-cibc-award.jpg)

## Years where nothing seems to move

Here's what doesn't make it into the photos.

There are seasons as an entrepreneur where you genuinely don't know if you're on the right path. You're sending proposals that don't close. You're showing up for people and nobody's clapping. You're doing community work at night after the paid work is done, and you start asking yourself whether any of it is adding up.

I've had years like that. Not weeks. Years.

When I pitched the Corex idea for the first time in 2014, I placed second. Twenty-five hundred dollars. I remember being grateful and disappointed in the same breath. I started with nothing. No investors, no family business, no safety net. Just a camera, a vision, and the stubborn belief that if I kept taking the next step, the road would eventually show itself.

That's what calculated steps means to me. You don't wait to see the whole staircase. You do the work in front of you, knowing better is coming, and you put one foot forward again tomorrow.

> For years it can look like nothing is happening. Then one weekend, everything changes.

I need anyone in that quiet season right now to hear this clearly: the seeds you're planting are not wasted because you can't see them yet.

![2026 BlackNorth Initiative Community Award honourees on stage](/images/writing/weekend-aligned-honourees.jpg)

## Why recognition still matters

Let me be honest about something. Nobody I know does community work for the plaque. The mentorship, the free sessions, the late-night calls with young creatives who need someone to tell them it's possible. You do it because it's right. Because that's what we're supposed to do for each other as people on this earth.

But I won't pretend these awards didn't mean something. When you're running hard for a long time, a moment of recognition works like a compass. It tells you you're pointed in the right direction. The 2026 Jamaican Diaspora Impact Award was for the community work, the part of my life that usually happens off the record. Having that seen by my own community, with my mother in the room, landed somewhere deep.

![Dwayne Holness with the 2026 Jamaican Diaspora Impact Award](/images/writing/weekend-aligned-jdia-award.jpg)

The BlackNorth Excellence Gala is a room I've always aspired to. What makes it even more personal: Corex produced the gala films for BlackNorth in 2023 and 2024. I've stood in that ballroom behind a camera, capturing other people's moments. This year I stood on the stage.

![Dwayne Holness with BlackNorth founder Wes Hall](/images/writing/weekend-aligned-wes-hall.jpg)

## Help people. It comes back around.

That's the part I want you to sit with.

You never know who you're serving today who'll be in a position to open a door for you tomorrow. The client you over-deliver for. The young person you answer a DM for. The church you serve on Sunday mornings when nobody's watching. I didn't serve on the KCLC media team to be recognized. I served because I believe in the house. And on Pastor Appreciation Day, the house turned around and said, "Thank you for being at the heart of our mission."

![Dwayne Holness with his Certificate of Appreciation at Kingsway Community Life Centre](/images/writing/weekend-aligned-kclc.jpg)

We have to keep helping one another. Not as a strategy. As a way of life. The returns don't come on your schedule, but they come.

![Dwayne Holness at the 2026 BlackNorth Initiative Excellence Gala](/images/writing/weekend-aligned-blacknorth.jpg)

To everyone who's watched what I do, who I've mentored, who I've built with, who quietly cheered from the side: this weekend was for you too. I came from nothing. I built from a vision. I'm proof that this thing is possible.

So keep stepping. Keep serving. Keep believing that better is coming, even on the days you can't see it.

Your weekend is on its way.
